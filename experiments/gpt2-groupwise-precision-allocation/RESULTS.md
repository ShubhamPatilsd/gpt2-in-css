# Groupwise coarse-precision allocation screen

## Result

Increasing rowwise coarse precision reduces the serial exact tail sharply
without changing GPT-2-small or its checkpoint:

| Uniform coarse precision | Mean groups | p95 | p99 | Maximum |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| q10 | 2.402 | 7 | 14 | 23 |
| q11 | 1.764 | 5 | 9 | 13 |
| q12 | 1.556 | 4 | 6 | 9 |

Static precision should be allocated by baseline tail-visit frequency, not by
mean bound tightening. Upgrading the 32 most frequently visited non-hot groups
to q12 gives mean 1.779 / p95 5 / p99 9 / max 14 before browser transport
allowance. Adding the measured 27-bucket q12 allowance gives mean 1.912 / p95
5 / p99 10 / max 16 in the production browser artifact.

## Transport finding

A naïve exact q12-minus-q10 correction needs 32-axis shards and 24 added
glyphs per upgraded row. That is not viable. Replacing selected q10 rows with
radix-64 high/residual q12 rows is much better: three 256-axis shards per
plane, six glyphs total per selected row, or three incremental glyphs over
q10. Both plane advances fit the stored and shaped uint16 ranges.

The resulting top-32 production page improves hard-tail browser latency by
about 22.5% while preserving the retained page's outputs on six fixtures. See
`../gpt2-groupwise-cauchy-blockflow-production-selective-q12-top32/RESULTS.md`.
